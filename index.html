Who We Are

Introduction

The Bhartiya Jan Seva Party is founded upon the ideals of the Aryavartha Thesis — a vision of enlightened governance, cultural integrity, and universal harmony rooted in the timeless principles of Dharma. The party believes that true leadership arises not from the pursuit of power, but from wisdom, discipline, humility, and selfless service to the nation. It seeks to build a society where governance is guided by ethics, justice, and compassion, and where progress is balanced with moral responsibility and spiritual consciousness.


Bhartiya Jan Seva Party stands for the preservation and renaissance of Bharat’s cultural heritage while embracing scientific advancement and modern innovation for the welfare of all humanity. The party envisions an India where education awakens conscience, citizens uphold dignity and duty, and development exists in harmony with nature and human values. Its mission is to create a Dharmic civilization founded upon truth, service, equality, cultural pride, and universal peace — making Bharat not merely a nation of prosperity, but a guiding light of consciousness and harmony for the world.

Foundation

The Three Pillars

Vidya — Knowledge

True governance must arise from knowledge. A civilization that invests in the awakening of minds over the accumulation of wealth shall stand eternal. Education is the lamp that banishes ignorance from the hearts of a nation.

Dharma — Righteousness

Dharma is the eternal law of harmony — the code of right conduct that binds society together. When rulers uphold Dharma and citizens live by it, justice flows naturally like a river from its source.

Seva — Service

Selfless service is the highest form of Dharma. A party rooted in Seva sees governance not as power but as responsibility — every citizen a stakeholder, every institution a guardian of the public trust.

Our Doctrine

The Aryavartha Thesis

A Doctrine of Enlightened Governance, Cultural Integrity, and Universal Harmony

The Aryavartha Thesis envisions a civilization where wisdom governs power, ethics guide progress, and humanity lives in harmony with both nature and the cosmos. Rooted in the timeless principles of Dharma, it seeks not merely to return to the past but to bring ancient clarity into the complexity of the modern age.


Governance, in this vision, is not the exercise of control but the expression of responsibility. True leadership must arise from knowledge (Vidya), restraint (Sanyam), and service (Seva). When power bows before wisdom and authority is tempered by humility, governance becomes an act of enlightened duty rather than domination.


Culture forms the soul of civilization — a living bridge between memory and imagination. To preserve culture is not to freeze it in time, but to refine and reinterpret it with every generation. The Aryavartha Thesis calls for a renaissance of art, language, attire, architecture, and civic manners that combine grace, simplicity, and dignity.


This philosophy affirms the ancient truth: Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam — humanity is one family. Science and spirituality are complementary paths to understanding reality. A harmonious civilization must integrate the insights of the Shastras with the discoveries of modern science, using both to serve all life.

Spiritual Lineage

The Idols of the Party

A living lineage of Acharyas whose wisdom and courage continue to guide the party’s Dharmic vision, presented in chronological order.

c. 788 – 820 CE
AdvaitaVidyaDharma

Jagadguru Adi Shankaracharya

Founder of Advaita Vedanta & the Four Peethas

Born in Kaladi, Kerala, Adi Shankaracharya is revered as the fountainhead of the Aryavartha vision itself — the sage who, in his brief life, unified the diverse philosophical currents of Bharat under the banner of Advaita Vedanta. Travelling the length of the subcontinent on foot, he debated scholars of every school and revived the study of the Vedas at a time when they had grown fragmented.


To safeguard this unity for all future generations, he established the four cardinal Peethas — Sringeri in the south, Dwarka in the west, Puri in the east, and Jyotir Math in the north — each entrusted to a disciple to preserve and transmit Vedic wisdom. His life remains the original template for the party’s belief that knowledge, humility, and tireless service are the true instruments of civilizational renewal.

1907 – 1982
DharmaTapasyaShastra

Dharm Samrat Swami Karpatri

(Swami Hariharananda Saraswati)

Born Har Narayan Ojha in Pratapgarh, Uttar Pradesh, he left worldly life at a young age and, after years of solitary discipline in the Himalayan caves, was initiated as a Dandi Sannyasi by Shankaracharya Brahmananda Saraswati of Jyotir Math. He came to be known as “Karpatri” for taking alms only in his cupped hands, and was honoured by the masses with the title Dharma Samrat — Emperor of Dharma.


In 1948 he founded the Akhil Bharatiya Ram Rajya Parishad, one of independent India’s earliest Dharma-based political movements, and led the campaigns for cow protection and against the Hindu Code Bill. A prolific scholar of the Vedas and Shastras, his life exemplifies the party’s conviction that fearless righteousness and deep learning must walk together.

1924 – 2022
SevaDharmaSahas

Jagadguru Shankaracharya
Swami Swaroopanand Saraswati

Shankaracharya of Dwarka-Sharda & Jyotir Math

Born Pothiram Upadhyay in the village of Dighori, Madhya Pradesh, he left home at the age of nine in pursuit of spiritual learning and studied under Swami Karpatri in Kashi. At eighteen, he joined the 1942 Quit India movement and became known as the “Revolutionary Sadhu” for his role in the freedom struggle.


A direct disciple of Brahmananda Saraswati, he was made Shankaracharya of Jyotir Math in 1973 and of Dwarka-Sharda Peeth in 1982, later serving as president of the Ram Rajya Parishad founded by his own Guru. Across nearly a century of public life he remained a tireless voice for Ram Janmabhoomi, the protection of the Ganga, and the cow protection movement, embodying a lifetime of Seva in service of Dharma.

Present Day
VidyaDharmaSanyam

Shri Mahamahim Jagadguru Vidyamaartand
Nigrahacharya

(Shree Bhagvatanand Guru)

The title Nigrahacharya is bestowed upon a scholar, teacher, or spiritual guide who specializes in nigraha — the discipline of control, correction, and protection of moral and social order. In traditional Indian philosophical and religious thought, a Nigrahacharya is regarded as a wise authority who guides people toward righteousness, self-discipline, truth, and ethical conduct.


Such a person is revered for deep knowledge of scriptures, strong leadership, and the ability to defend principles against injustice or corruption. The role of a Nigrahacharya is not only to teach spiritual wisdom but also to inspire society to follow duty, culture, and Dharma with courage and integrity — embodying the living ideal of the Aryavartha Thesis.


As the spiritual and philosophical idol of the Bhartiya Jan Seva Party in the present age, Nigrahacharya Ji represents the highest aspiration of the party: that governance should be inspired by the same wisdom, compassion, and fearless righteousness that has characterized this lineage of Dharmic sages from Adi Shankaracharya onward.

Our Commitment

Party Manifesto

Society & Culture
  • Promote national unity and cultural cohesion across all regions and communities of India.
  • Eliminate caste-based discrimination through economically targeted affirmative action.
  • Adopt zero-tolerance policy toward violent and organized crime with full victim support services.
  • Promote cultural harmony and balance within India’s heritage, emphasizing ethical governance and social equity.
Defence & Security
  • Strengthen and indigenize Armed Forces to ensure robust defensive capabilities and strategic deterrence.
  • Bolster conventional defence infrastructure and invest in advanced civilian technologies for peaceful energy and space exploration.
  • Foster comprehensive relations with neighboring countries characterized by mutual respect and non-aggression.
  • Support non-proliferation initiatives and international peace frameworks.
Education & Research
  • Revitalize educational excellence through strategic reforms in schools, universities, and vocational training institutions.
  • Enable India to re-emerge as a global hub for learning, innovation, and knowledge production.
  • Integrate Shastraic wisdom with modern scientific inquiry in research institutions.
  • Prioritize moral education alongside technical skill-building across all levels of schooling.
Economy & Industry
  • Provide incentives for ‘Make in India’ within automobile and automotive component sectors.
  • Adopt strategic import substitution in sectors where export-led growth is feasible.
  • Ensure full and decent employment through MSME support and entrepreneurship incentives.
  • Diversify trade partnerships and facilitate trade through transparent policy frameworks.
Environment & Nature
  • Development must exist in harmony with nature — no prosperity at the cost of ecological destruction.
  • Promote renewable energy and sustainable agriculture rooted in traditional Indian land stewardship.
  • Protect India’s rivers, forests, and biodiversity as sacred national assets.
  • Integrate environmental responsibility into all public policy and urban planning.
Governance & Law
  • Implement e-governance initiatives and ensure independent oversight of law enforcement institutions.
  • Undertake comprehensive police reforms prioritizing accountability, training, and community trust.
  • Any proposal regarding a change in the country’s name proceeds only after thorough democratic and constitutional consultation.
  • Strengthen anti-corruption frameworks with transparent, citizen-accessible enforcement mechanisms.
