Who We Are

Introduction

The Bhartiya Jan Seva Party is founded upon the ideals of the Aryavartha Thesis — a vision of enlightened governance, cultural integrity, and universal harmony rooted in the timeless principles of Dharma. The party believes that true leadership arises not from the pursuit of power, but from wisdom, discipline, humility, and selfless service to the nation. It seeks to build a society where governance is guided by ethics, justice, and compassion, and where progress is balanced with moral responsibility and spiritual consciousness.


Bhartiya Jan Seva Party stands for the preservation and renaissance of Bharat’s cultural heritage while embracing scientific advancement and modern innovation for the welfare of all humanity. The party envisions an India where education awakens conscience, citizens uphold dignity and duty, and development exists in harmony with nature and human values. Its mission is to create a Dharmic civilization founded upon truth, service, equality, cultural pride, and universal peace — making Bharat not merely a nation of prosperity, but a guiding light of consciousness and harmony for the world.

Foundation

The Three Pillars

Vidya — Knowledge

True governance must arise from knowledge. A civilization that invests in the awakening of minds over the accumulation of wealth shall stand eternal. Education is the lamp that banishes ignorance from the hearts of a nation.

Dharma — Righteousness

Dharma is the eternal law of harmony — the code of right conduct that binds society together. When rulers uphold Dharma and citizens live by it, justice flows naturally like a river from its source.

Seva — Service

Selfless service is the highest form of Dharma. A party rooted in Seva sees governance not as power but as responsibility — every citizen a stakeholder, every institution a guardian of the public trust.

Our Doctrine

The Aryavartha Thesis

A Doctrine of Enlightened Governance, Cultural Integrity, and Universal Harmony

The Aryavartha Thesis envisions a civilization where wisdom governs power, ethics guide progress, and humanity lives in harmony with both nature and the cosmos. Rooted in the timeless principles of Dharma, it seeks not merely to return to the past but to bring ancient clarity into the complexity of the modern age.


Governance, in this vision, is not the exercise of control but the expression of responsibility. True leadership must arise from knowledge (Vidya), restraint (Sanyam), and service (Seva). When power bows before wisdom and authority is tempered by humility, governance becomes an act of enlightened duty rather than domination.


Culture forms the soul of civilization — a living bridge between memory and imagination. To preserve culture is not to freeze it in time, but to refine and reinterpret it with every generation. The Aryavartha Thesis calls for a renaissance of art, language, attire, architecture, and civic manners that combine grace, simplicity, and dignity.


This philosophy affirms the ancient truth: Vasudhaiva Kutumbakam — humanity is one family. Science and spirituality are complementary paths to understanding reality. A harmonious civilization must integrate the insights of the Shastras with the discoveries of modern science, using both to serve all life.

Jagadgurus of the Party

The Idols of the Party

A living lineage of Acharyas whose wisdom and courage continue to guide the party’s Dharmic vision, presented in the order of the guru lineage.

Eternal · Adi Guru
VidyaDharmaSanatana

Jagadguru Narayan

The Primordial Guru of the Vedic Tradition

In the Vedic and Vaishnava traditions, Bhagavan Narayana is revered as the Adi Guru — the original source of sacred knowledge, from whom the teaching is held to descend through Brahma and the great sages to every generation of teachers that followed.


He stands at the head of this lineage as the symbol of knowledge that is eternal, selfless and given for the welfare of all beings — the ideal that the Bhartiya Jan Seva Party places at the root of Vidya, Dharma and Seva.

c. 788 – 820 CE
AdvaitaVidyaDharma

Jagadguru Adi Shankaracharya

Founder of Advaita Vedanta & the Four Peethas

Born in Kaladi, Kerala, Adi Shankaracharya is revered as the fountainhead of the Aryavartha vision itself — the sage who, in his brief life, unified the diverse philosophical currents of Bharat under the banner of Advaita Vedanta. Travelling the length of the subcontinent on foot, he debated scholars of every school and revived the study of the Vedas at a time when they had grown fragmented.


To safeguard this unity for all future generations, he established the four cardinal Peethas — Sringeri in the south, Dwarka in the west, Puri in the east, and Jyotir Math in the north — each entrusted to a disciple to preserve and transmit Vedic wisdom. His life remains the original template for the party’s belief that knowledge, humility, and tireless service are the true instruments of civilizational renewal.

c. 1017 – 1137 CE
BhaktiVishishtadvaitaSeva

Jagadguru Ramanujacharya

Founder of Vishishtadvaita Vedanta & Sri Vaishnavism

Born in Sriperumbudur, in present-day Tamil Nadu, Ramanujacharya is remembered as the great teacher of Vishishtadvaita, the philosophy of qualified non-duality, which holds that the soul and the world are real and are bound to the Supreme through love and devotion. His Sri Bhashya, a commentary on the Brahma Sutras, remains a cornerstone of Vedantic scholarship.


Based at the Srirangam temple, he reorganised its worship and administration and travelled widely to spread his teaching. Tradition remembers him for opening the path of devotion and spiritual knowledge to people of every background, an example of the party’s belief that wisdom must serve everyone and that dignity belongs to all.

c. 1238 – 1317 CE
DvaitaBhaktiVidya

Jagadguru Madhvacharya

Founder of Dvaita Vedanta & the Udupi Ashta Mathas

Born near Udupi in present-day Karnataka, Madhvacharya is the great teacher of Dvaita Vedanta, the philosophy of duality, which affirms the eternal distinction between the individual soul and the Supreme and places devotion to Lord Vishnu at the heart of spiritual life. He wrote commentaries on the Brahma Sutras, the Upanishads and the Bhagavad Gita, and debated scholars of other schools with great rigour.


He established the Sri Krishna Matha at Udupi and the tradition of the eight Mathas that carry on its worship and learning to this day. His life shows the party’s conviction that clear reasoning, firm devotion and the careful preservation of institutions must go together.

Shri Mahamahim Jagadguru Vidyamaartand
Nigrahacharya

(Shree Bhagvatanand Guru)

Nigrahacharya Shri Bhagavatananda Guru is a contemporary Hindu religious teacher associated with the Nigraha Sampradaya. He is described by his followers as a religious preacher, scholar, philanthropist, and speaker with knowledge of Sanatana Vedic scriptures. The title “Nigrahacharya” is linked to the idea of restraining or correcting unrighteous conduct, and his tradition provides disciples with guidance on initiation and conduct through texts such as Shishya Patheya.

Our Commitment

Party Manifesto

Society & Culture
  • Promote national unity and cultural cohesion across all regions and communities of India.
  • Eliminate caste-based discrimination through economically targeted affirmative action.
  • Adopt zero-tolerance policy toward violent and organized crime with full victim support services.
  • Promote cultural harmony and balance within India’s heritage, emphasizing ethical governance and social equity.
Defence & Security
  • Strengthen and indigenize Armed Forces to ensure robust defensive capabilities and strategic deterrence.
  • Bolster conventional defence infrastructure and invest in advanced civilian technologies for peaceful energy and space exploration.
  • Foster comprehensive relations with neighboring countries characterized by mutual respect and non-aggression.
  • Support non-proliferation initiatives and international peace frameworks.
Education & Research
  • Revitalize educational excellence through strategic reforms in schools, universities, and vocational training institutions.
  • Enable India to re-emerge as a global hub for learning, innovation, and knowledge production.
  • Integrate Shastraic wisdom with modern scientific inquiry in research institutions.
  • Prioritize moral education alongside technical skill-building across all levels of schooling.
Economy & Industry
  • Provide incentives for ‘Make in India’ within automobile and automotive component sectors.
  • Adopt strategic import substitution in sectors where export-led growth is feasible.
  • Ensure full and decent employment through MSME support and entrepreneurship incentives.
  • Diversify trade partnerships and facilitate trade through transparent policy frameworks.
Environment & Nature
  • Development must exist in harmony with nature — no prosperity at the cost of ecological destruction.
  • Promote renewable energy and sustainable agriculture rooted in traditional Indian land stewardship.
  • Protect India’s rivers, forests, and biodiversity as sacred national assets.
  • Integrate environmental responsibility into all public policy and urban planning.
Governance & Law
  • Implement e-governance initiatives and ensure independent oversight of law enforcement institutions.
  • Undertake comprehensive police reforms prioritizing accountability, training, and community trust.
  • Any proposal regarding a change in the country’s name proceeds only after thorough democratic and constitutional consultation.
  • Strengthen anti-corruption frameworks with transparent, citizen-accessible enforcement mechanisms.
Our Aims

Journey to Renaissance

Nine steps on the path to a Dharmic civilization

1
People’s Mentality Development & Massive Shift

A nationwide awakening of thought, where citizens move from distraction and division towards duty, discipline, self-respect and conscious living.

2
Nature Restoration

Revive rivers, forests, soil and biodiversity, and bring back the traditional land stewardship that kept Bharat in balance with nature.

3
Restoration of Scriptures, Culture & Principles

Protect, study and renew the Shastras, customs and principles of Dharma so that they live again in everyday family and public life.

4
Infrastructure Development

Build roads, rail, water, power, housing and digital networks that reach every village and city, designed to last and to respect the land.

5
Technology Inspired by Harappa & Ancient Civilizations

Learn from the town planning, water management, metallurgy and craft science of the Harappan and other ancient civilizations, and bring that wisdom into modern invention.

6
Shows & Serials on Faith and Social Problems

Produce television and streaming stories that portray religious and societal problems, and the forces of division and deceit behind them, so that the public learns to recognise them.

7
Documentaries on the Reality of the World

Make investigative documentaries on secretive organisations and hidden influence in religious fields, and on the links between politicians and religious preachers, based on verified facts and open evidence.

8
Upbringing of Great Religious Scholars

Support gurukuls, scholarships and teacher lineages to raise a new generation of learned, fearless and humble scholars of Dharma.

9
Great Recitations of Shastriya Topics

Hold large public recitations and discourses by scholars on the Shastras, in the languages of the people, to spread awareness in every region.

Livelihood

Employment Model

Work as Seva: the state leads where public trust is essential, and works with private enterprise where creativity and industry can flourish together.

Governmental Rule

1

Societies & Apartments

Government oversight of housing societies and apartment complexes for fair maintenance, safety and clean, green living, creating jobs in management, security, and civic services.

2

Social Media

A national, accountable framework for social platforms that protects citizens from fraud, hate and misinformation, with openings for moderators, analysts and cyber-safety experts.

3

School Collaboration

Close partnership between government, schools and local industry, bringing apprenticeships, vocational courses and mentors into the classroom.

4

Food Production

State-supported farming, storage, processing and fair-price distribution, giving steady work to farmers, cooperatives, and food-processing units.

5

Arms & Ammunition

Indigenous, government-controlled manufacture of defence equipment, building high-skill employment in engineering, metallurgy and research.

6

Hospitals

A strong network of public hospitals and health centres, with employment for doctors, nurses, technicians, and traditional healers alongside modern medicine.

7

Architectural Designers

State-guided design of public buildings and towns that blend Indian architectural principles with modern engineering, opening careers for architects, planners and craftsmen.

8

Telecom

Public ownership and oversight of telecom networks to give affordable, secure connectivity everywhere, with work for engineers, tower crews and service staff.

9

Educational Institutions

Government-run and regulated schools, colleges and universities that combine moral education with technical skill, giving stable careers to teachers, researchers and administrators.

Government & Private Collaboration

1

Gaming

Government and private studios together build Indian-themed, value-based games, giving young developers, artists and storytellers a home industry.

2

Animation

Shared studios and training centres create animation based on Indian history and Dharmic stories for children and global audiences.

3

Weapon Training (Indian Culture)

Government-recognised academies preserve and teach traditional Indian martial arts and Shastra Vidya, training instructors and champions in discipline and self-defence.

4

Laptop Making

Joint manufacturing units make laptops in India, creating jobs in design, assembly, testing and supply chains.

5

Phone Making

Government incentives and private enterprise work together to produce phones and components in India, building a full domestic electronics ecosystem.

6

Cinema

Partnerships that fund and promote meaningful Indian cinema, supporting writers, directors, actors, and technicians while upholding cultural dignity.

Every hand employed, every skill honoured, since work done with dignity is itself a form of service.
